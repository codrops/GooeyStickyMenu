# AGENTS.md

Notes for coding agents working on this repo. Read [README.md](README.md) first for what the demo is.

## Setup

- There is no build step, no package.json and nothing to install. Plain HTML, CSS and ES modules.
- Serve the root with any static server (`npx serve .` or `python3 -m http.server`). ES modules don't load from `file://`.
- GSAP, ScrollTrigger, ScrollSmoother and SplitText are vendored in `js/` and loaded as classic scripts before the module, so they're globals. Don't import them.
- There are no automated tests. Check a change in a browser on every page: scroll slowly and quickly past the hero, every section word and every picture (centred, grazing an end of the menu, across one half of it, the pair, the products), scroll back up, resize to phone width, and look at the console. Check it with reduced motion too.
- The shader's cost only shows on a real GPU, at a retina-sized window: headless Chromium's software renderer is too slow to tell. Measure the frame rate with a title's strands out, which is the heaviest moment.

## How the page works

- **Two layers in step.** The page scrolls through ScrollSmoother. The canvas (`.goo`) sits at the top of `#smooth-content`, and each frame `js/index.js` moves it down by as much as the page has scrolled, so it stays on the screen, and redraws it from `gsap.ticker` right after ScrollSmoother moves the content, so the pictures in the canvas never drift from the page. It only draws when the frame would look different from the last one drawn (`Goo.unchanged()`: every number the shader reads, near enough not to show, and the titles' textures), so a page at rest costs the GPU nothing. The canvas has the screen's resolution, twice its CSS size at most, within `MAX_PIXELS` (8 million): a larger screen draws the liquid a little softer rather than more slowly. That's why the effect only runs with the smoother. Being inside the page lets the frame and a hero's text (`z-index: 16`) sit above the canvas (`15`) while everything else is under it; the menu and the settings panel are outside the page, above both. The menu comes first in the markup, so the keyboard reaches it before the page. The first frame is drawn before `loading` comes off, so nothing appears late; there are no intro animations.
- **The canvas draws the pictures, the chapter titles and the menu's shape.** The `<img>` elements and the headings stay in the page for layout and for what they say, hidden by `.is-goo` on `body`; the menu's own background is hidden by `.is-drawn`. The labels stay real links above the canvas.
- **Layout.** `measure()` reads each `.goo-picture` box in page coordinates and the menu's box on every ScrollTrigger refresh; each frame only subtracts the scroll. `--menu-hw` (half the menu's width) is set on `refreshInit`, and the looks are sized and placed from it: a look is 180px wider than the menu, so the menu's ends sit inside a look it crosses. Every look touches the menu as it passes: `.look--c` goes straight through it, `.look--l` grazes its left end (15px under it), `.look--pr` crosses its right half, `.look-pair` puts one on each end. The looks start below their chapter's title, never over it: a letter under a picture would still hold a strand.
- **The liquid.** The menu is a capsule and each strand two tapered halves that meet in a smooth waist, blended into one distance field with a cubic smooth minimum (`liquidAt()`). The wide blend with the menu makes a strand flare into it. At the other end the strand opens into a foot on what it holds (`sdHeld()`): the closer to that thing's outline, the wider it is, and it's cut flat against the outline, a little over the rim. The foot is measured from the outline, so it follows the edge it sits on, and it only widens on the strand's side of the point it holds, so it doesn't climb the thing's other edges. Under the menu's own surface the strand isn't cut by the thing's edge, and it adds to the menu's depth only as far as it blends with the menu (`surfaced()`): a strand that lies inside the menu is liquid in liquid, and leaves no fold or glint there. A snapped half ends in a drop: the last stretch of it swells, round at the tip (`sdStrand()`'s `bead`). The pictures are boxes underneath the liquid (`sdPicture()`). Their corners are round where the menu is near, and round the point a strand holds on to, for as long as there's liquid on it: as a snapped half draws back in, the corner goes square with it. `closestToBox()` in `js/strands.js` finds the strand's point on that same rounded outline, so a strand always ends on the picture. Up to `MAX` (8) pictures are drawn at once: those within a quarter screen of the viewport.
- **Strands.** The liquid holds two kinds of thing, each with its own `Strands`: the pictures, and the parts of the titles' letters. It sticks to a thing only once the two have touched; it never reaches across a gap. From then on a strand runs from the thing's nearest point to a root inside the menu, deeper than the strand is thick. So while the two overlap, the strand lies inside the menu and nothing shows, and it leaves the menu's outline alone until the thing's point nears that outline (the shader skips it until then). As the thing's edge or corner comes out of the menu it brings the liquid out with it, with no gap and nothing that has to grow first, the same in both scroll directions. The strand gets thinner in the middle and narrower at the foot the longer it gets; on a picture's corner the foot is narrower still. It's thinnest, and breaks, at `breakAt` along it from its higher end (the middle, or near the top for a thread pulled upwards). Over the last of its stretch a thick strand necks down there (to `neck`), so it parts in a point; a material that should break in a cut (set caramel) sets `neck` as thick as its strand; it snaps past `length` half-heights (shorter when scrolling fast; the page's speed is evened out over `EVEN` seconds first). All the distances are in units of the menu's half height.
- **After a snap.** Each half starts as the strand was when it broke (`snap`) and draws back on the `tip` spring. What it gives up in length gathers in a drop at its tip (`bead`), which goes back into what the half hangs from over the last of the way. The menu's half draws the menu's surface up less as it shortens; once its tip is under the surface, what's left is a mound, and that levels out in its own time (`level`, the strand's `mound`). The strand is dropped only when the mound has no height left. A thing that touches the menu again while its snapped strand is still drawing back is taken hold of once the halves are home (`settled`). While a strand holds, the menu leans towards it, and a picture's pixels near it can be pulled towards it (`pull`, 0 on every page: pictures stay still); after the snap the pixels settle and the menu wobbles (`MenuMotion`, which adds up what every `Strands` does to the menu; a snap's kick reaches it over `KICK` seconds).
- **Cutting a strand.** With a mouse or a trackpad, a quick stroke across a strand in the last of its stretch breaks it where the stroke crossed it (`CUT` in `js/index.js`: from 80% of the way to snapping by itself, at 600 CSS px a second or faster; only outside the menu). Each frame `js/index.js` takes the stroke from where the pointer was the frame before to where it is, and every `Strands` cuts what it crosses (`cutAcross()`), before `MenuMotion` takes in the frame's snaps. The hint: while the mouse is within `CUT.near` (20px) of a strand that could be cut now, outside the menu (`canCutNear()`), `html` has `is-cuttable` and the cursor is a crosshair, whatever is under it; nothing else shows or moves. A cut and a snap are the same break (`part()`), at the waist when the strand snaps by itself and wherever it's cut: each piece starts as just that stretch of the strand was, so nothing changes in the frame it breaks. A piece cut away from the waist keeps it inside it, or has it beyond its tip on the other piece: each half carries where along it it's thinnest and how thin (`neck`, `uNeck…` in the shader, which `sdStrand()` eases to and out of), and the waist fills out as the piece draws back. Not on touch screens, where a finger scrolls the page: there `CUTS` (`js/goo.js`) is false, the shader is compiled without the neck uniforms (`NECK()` gives a strand its waist at its tips) and keeps to fewer uniforms.
- **Extra threads.** A sticky material pulls more than one thread from a thing (`SETTINGS.strings`, up to two more, and a fraction for a thinner one: aloe has 0.8, one extra thread at four fifths of its thickness; caramel's "pull" stage 2). Each picture and each part of a letter gets two further strands with their own `Strands` (`strings`, `letterStrings` in `js/index.js`): one to each side of its strand, `EXTRA` menu half-heights away along a picture's outline (`closestAlongBox()`) or aside on a letter (`closestBesidePart()`), thinner than the strand (`scale`), so they break before it, one after the other. An extra thread on a letter keeps to the spot it took hold of: while it holds, it only slides along the outline from there (`pin`). How much of an extra thread there is follows the setting (`share`), so the threads come and go gradually as the material changes. The pictures' extra threads are drawn from their own uniforms (`uString…`, up to `MAX_STRINGS`), each on its picture's outline; the letters' go in with the letters' strands.
- **Nothing in one frame.** Every change to the liquid's shape is gradual: the swell towards a thing that has just touched grows with the grip, a strand's thickness carries over a snap whatever caused it, a mound levels before its strand goes. Keep it that way: a value that sets the shape (a radius, the blend with the menu, the foot) must never be reset while it still shows.
- **Letters.** For each title, `letterShapes()` in `js/letters.js` draws the text once at one pixel per CSS px and takes two things from it: the distance to the letters' outline (a float texture, `sdTitle()` in the shader, which a strand's foot is cut against) and the outline's points. A letter is one part, or one part per foot when it stands on several (the runs of ink just above its lowest point: an M, an A, an R have two). Each part holds one strand, at its outline point nearest the menu (`closestToPart()`); where several are as near, the one closest to where the strand last held on. A strand's thickness, its foot and its flare into the menu scale with the letters' stroke width (`scale`), so small letters pull thin threads. Only a title on screen has strands drawn, up to `MAX_LETTERS` (16), the thickest first.
- **The material.** The liquid is light glass with a surface that rises from its rim and levels out (`surface()`); its slope is measured across a few pixels, which keeps the light smooth over the middle of thin parts. Everything under it is seen one way (`frosted()`): shifted towards the middle like under a lens, out of focus, and under a thin white veil that keeps the labels legible. The lens bends by how much liquid lies behind the rim: a strand, or its foot on a picture's edge, hardly shifts what's under it, so a picture doesn't show squeezed inside a foot. A picture's edge is blurred as wide as the rest, so it fades into the page. Where nothing lies under the liquid, the canvas stays see-through and the page's own background shows. Light falls from the upper left: a bright edge and a sheen on the rim that faces it, a darker rim on the far side, and a soft shadow on the page and the pictures.
- **Section words.** They're `.chapter__title`s. The canvas draws them too, so the liquid frosts them like the pictures and holds on to their letters: on every refresh `measure()` draws each `.chapter__title` into a texture with the page's own font, size, baseline, letter-spacing (`fillSpaced()`, letter by letter where the text before each one ends, so kerning stays) and case, in its `--color-title`, and builds its letters' shape. The headings stay in the page, transparent (`.is-goo`), so a title the canvas doesn't draw is missing: two can be on screen together (the end of one section and the start of the next), so the canvas draws up to `MAX_TITLES` (2), each with its own texture units (1–2 and 3–4). A title that has left the screen still counts as on it while the liquid holds one of its letters: the menu sits near the screen's lower edge, and a long strand to a title just below it must not be cut there.
- **Materials.** What the liquid is made of sets both its look and its motion. The defaults in `SETTINGS` and `GLASS` are clear glass; a page names a material on its `body` (`data-material`), and `applyMaterial()` writes that material's values over the defaults, in place, before anything runs. A material lists only what it changes. Its labels' colours, and the menu's own colour where there's no canvas, are in `css/base.css` under `[data-material=…]`. A material can also change as the page scrolls (caramel): it lists `stages` in place of fixed values, one for the page's opening and one per chapter, each with the same keys and the colour of the menu's labels over it (`labels`). `js/index.js` works out the stage from the scroll (`stageAt()`): the first until a chapter's title reaches the menu, turning into the next while that title passes under the menu, from its top reaching the menu's foot to its foot leaving the menu's top. So a title's letters and its chapter's pictures pull on that chapter's material, and the strands of the chapter before are left alone until the next title arrives. `blendStages()` mixes the two stages the page is between (springs by ratio, everything else by difference) and writes the result into `SETTINGS` and `GLASS` in place, whenever the stage changes; the labels' colour goes on the menu as `--color-menu-link` and `--color-menu-link-hover`, changing late between two stages, once the liquid is nearly the next one's colour. Because the stage follows the scroll's position, scrolling back undoes it.
- **The liquid's colour.** Each colour of light is absorbed by its own amount, the more the deeper the liquid (`GLASS.tint` at full depth, `depth` for how strongly), so a thin thread is pale and the menu's middle is deep. The canvas can only lay colour over the page, not tint it, so `filtered()` lays the darkest colour's share exactly and uses the page's own background colour (`uGround`, read from `body` on start) for the rest. The other variations at the end of the page sit on a ground of their own, `--color-others` (black; 01's a very dark grey, `#1f2224`, so its clear red menu still shows red over it), which also runs on below the page's end and fills the browser's bounce past it: `js/index.js` passes their box and colour (`dark`, `uDarkRect`, `uDarkGround`), and there the shader takes that as the ground. A material whose labels are dark gives light ones for over that black (`--color-menu-link-on-dark` and `--color-menu-link-hover-on-dark`, only aloe's), and its labels turn as far as the black has come up under the menu, with the scroll. So `--color-bg` must be the ground under the menu: where the ground is a gradient fixed to the screen (honey, aloe), it's the gradient's bottom colour, which is why the gradient is fixed to the screen and not the page. The same filter colours the shadow: `caustic` is how much of the light that came through the liquid lands in it.
- **Settings.** The values the effect is tuned by live in two objects: `SETTINGS` in `js/strands.js` (the strands' shape and motion, including the springs) and `GLASS` in `js/goo.js` (the blend with the menu and the material's look, sent to the shader as uniforms every frame). `js/panel.js` builds a control for each from its `GROUPS` list (sliders, and colour pickers for colours) and writes straight into those objects. The panel always starts closed; "copy values" puts both on the clipboard as JSON, "reset" goes back to the values the page loaded with, its material's. A new setting needs its entry in one of the two objects and a row in `GROUPS`. On a page whose material has stages, the panel's controls follow the values as they change (`panel.sync()`), a tweak holds until the stage next changes, and "reset" goes back to the stage's own values.
- **Pictures of any shape.** Each image fills a whole layer of the texture array, stretched if it's another shape (the layer is as large as the page's largest image). `Goo.fit()` works out, per picture and per frame, the part of its image that shows (`uFit`): like `object-fit: cover`, kept towards `data-focus-x` on narrow screens (the DOM image gets the same through `--focus-x`), and zoomed in for a parallax. Where the liquid drags a picture's content towards the menu (`uPull`, only when the panel's "picture pull" is above 0), the drag eases off towards the picture's far side, which stays put, so a small picture (the footer's) never shows from beyond its edge.
- **The hero.** A shop page opens on `.hero`: its photograph is a `.goo-picture` with `data-parallax`, so the canvas shows it 25% larger than its box and a ScrollTrigger moves what shows down inside it as the hero scrolls away, slower than the page. The name, what the product is (two lines, `.hero__about`, the line break set per page) and the buy line sit over it, above the canvas, in one column at the bottom left (`.hero__info`), and don't move on their own. The buy line (price and "Add to bag", 20px apart) is as tall as the menu and level with it, beside it; it's narrow enough to clear the menu down to the phone layout, where the whole column sits 60px above the menu's top. Nothing in the hero may touch the menu at any width: check a change from 320px to 1920px wide. While the hero is under the settings button, `body.is-over-hero` turns the button light.
- **Fallback.** With reduced motion, or without WebGL2, there's no smoother, no canvas and no settings panel: the images, the headings and the menu's own light background show as plain HTML and CSS.

## Adding a variation

1. Add the material to `MATERIALS` in `js/materials.js`, with only the values it changes and a comment on how it looks and moves.
2. Add its labels' colours and the menu's own colour under `[data-material='<name>']` in `css/base.css`.
3. Copy a page to `index<N>.html` and change `data-material`, its title and description (each in three tags: "<Name> Gooey Sticky Menu | Codrops", and what that page's material does, ending "Made with WebGL and GSAP. Part of Codrops' Gooey Sticky Menu."), and the canonical and `og:url` to that page. A shop page links `css/shop.css` and its own colours, `css/<material>.css`, after `base.css`, and preloads its hero image. A material with stages needs as many chapters as it has stages after the first.
4. Add it, by its one-word name (Jelly, Honey, Aloe, Caramel: what the menu is made of), to the `.frame__demos` nav on **every** page. The current page's entry is a `<span class="frame__demo frame__demo--current" aria-current="page">`, not a link.
5. Add it to the `.others` band at the end of every other page: its hero photograph, with its `data-focus-x`, and its name, linked to its page. Each page shows the other variations there, so with five variations the row needs a fourth column.

## Conventions

- Raw WebGL2 and GLSL ES 3.00, no three.js. The shader is a template string in `js/goo.js`.
- Positions in the shader are CSS px with y down, like the page; `uDpr` turns them into device pixels for the 1px edge.
- The pictures live in one `TEXTURE_2D_ARRAY` on texture unit 0 (mipmapped, a layer as large as the page's largest image), indexed by their order in the page; the title on screen is a `TEXTURE_2D` on unit 1 and its letters' distance field an `R16F` texture on unit 2 (`precision highp sampler2D`, or its values lose their range). Sample with `textureLod`, never `texture`: the picture is picked per pixel, and implicit derivatives aren't reliable there.
- Don't use GLSL ES 3.00 keywords or reserved words as names (`flat`, `smooth`, `sample`, `cast`, `filter`, `input`, `output`…).
- A strand only counts for pixels within reach of it (`within()` in the shader), and the shadow is only worked out near the liquid. Keep both when adding to `liquidAt()`: it runs several times per pixel.
- The springs (`tip`, `settle`, `squash`, `sway` in `SETTINGS`) are stiffness and damping per second, stepped with the frame's time (capped at 1/30s). Damping at or above twice the square root of the stiffness means no overshoot (aloe's and honey's snapped halves); below it, they swing past and back.
- Text is 15px Host Grotesk, line-height 1.4; the big type is set in `css/shop.css`.
- Spacing comes only from the scale in `:root` (`--s-15` to `--s-240`, and `--s-5` inside a component); the menu's ends are `--s-30` plus `--s-15`.
- Nothing animates unless it was asked for: no intro animations, no fades on load. What does move: the pictures' parallax (the hero's image a quarter larger than its box, drifting from the top of the page until it has left the screen; each section picture's, `.look__img`, `LOOK_ZOOM` larger, drifting from the moment it comes into view until it has left; the boxes themselves never move, so the liquid meets the same edges), and the statement, whose lines rise out of their masks one after another every time it comes into view scrolling down (SplitText, `autoSplit` so a resize splits it again; put back out of sight once it has left the screen at the bottom, scrolling up; shown as it is when scrolled into from below or when the page opens past it; not with reduced motion).

## Style

- Two-space indentation, single quotes, semicolons, trailing commas. Follow what's in the file.
- Comments say what a part looks like or why it's there, in plain words.
- No dependencies and no build tooling unless asked.

## Ask before changing

- The pages' `<title>`, meta description, the `frame__title` heading and the tags.
- The frame's links (no article: "All demos" and "GitHub"), the images, `og-image.jpg` (caramel at "Pull") and the credits.
